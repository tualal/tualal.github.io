I. ORIGIN

The Nebula

Somewhere in the dark between galaxies, a cloud of hydrogen and helium begins to stir. Gravity pulls dust and gas inward — slowly at first, then faster. A new star is being born inside a swirling nursery millions of miles wide.

This has been happening for 13 billion years. It will keep happening long after we are gone.

II. MATURITY

Main Sequence

Pressure builds until nuclear fusion ignites at the core. Hydrogen slams into hydrogen and releases an unimaginable amount of energy. The star finds its balance — gravity pulling in, radiation pushing out.

Our sun has been in this phase for 4.6 billion years. It has another 5 billion to go. This is the long, quiet middle of a star's life.

III. EXPANSION

Red Giant

The hydrogen in the core runs out. Fusion slows. Gravity wins for a moment — and the core collapses inward while the outer layers balloon outward to a size that swallows entire planets.

The star turns red. It grows so large that if it were our sun, it would engulf Mercury, Venus, and Earth. Everything we've ever known, gone.

IV. COLLAPSE

Supernova

The core collapses in less than a second. The resulting shockwave tears the star apart in an explosion so bright it can outshine an entire galaxy. For a few weeks, one dying star puts out more light than 100 billion suns.

The elements forged in its core — carbon, oxygen, iron — scatter across space. They will eventually form new stars, new planets, new life. You are made of this.

V. AFTERMATH

The Remnant

What's left depends on how massive the star was. Smaller stars leave behind a white dwarf — a dim, dense ember cooling slowly over billions of years. Massive stars leave something stranger: a neutron star, or a black hole so dense that even light cannot escape.

Either way, the story isn't over. The material scattered by the explosion seeds the next generation. Every atom in your body was forged in a star that died before our solar system existed.

We are, all of us, made of stardust.
