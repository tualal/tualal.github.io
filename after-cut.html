Grocery Shopping on a Budget

How to eat well on a cut without spending a lot of money.

The Key Principle

Eating in a calorie deficit does not mean eating expensive "diet food." The cheapest groceries at most stores — eggs, oats, rice, beans, frozen vegetables, chicken thighs — are also some of the best foods for a cut.

Budget tip: Build your meals around cheap protein sources and fill the rest with whole foods. Avoid pre-packaged "diet" products — they're usually expensive and not that filling.

Best Budget Protein Sources

  • Eggs — cheap, versatile, high protein
  • Canned tuna or sardines — extremely cheap per gram of protein
  • Chicken thighs (bone-in) — cheaper than breast, still high protein
  • Ground turkey — usually affordable, easy to cook in bulk
  • Cottage cheese and Greek yogurt — solid protein per dollar
  • Dried lentils and beans — very cheap, also high fiber

Filling Carb Sources

  • White or brown rice — bulk bags are dirt cheap
  • Oats — very cheap, filling, and high fiber
  • Potatoes and sweet potatoes — extremely filling per calorie
  • Bread (whole grain) — easy to portion, cheap

Vegetables on a Budget

Frozen vegetables are just as nutritious as fresh and way cheaper. Frozen broccoli, spinach, mixed veggies, and green beans are all great options. They also have a long shelf life so nothing goes to waste.

Sample Weekly Grocery List (~$50)

  • 2 dozen eggs
  • 2 lbs ground turkey
  • 2 lbs chicken thighs
  • 2 cans tuna
  • Large bag of rice
  • Large container of oats
  • 3–4 bags frozen vegetables
  • Greek yogurt (large container)
  • Cottage cheese
  • Bananas and apples
