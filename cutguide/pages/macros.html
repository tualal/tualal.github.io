Understanding Macros

Protein, carbs, and fat — what actually matters during a cut.

What Are Macros?

Macros (macronutrients) are the three main categories of calories: protein, carbohydrates, and fat. Each gram has a calorie value: protein and carbs are 4 cal/g, fat is 9 cal/g.

During a cut, total calories are the most important variable. Macros matter second, with protein being the only one that's non-negotiable.

Protein — The Priority

Protein is what keeps your muscle intact while you're in a deficit. If you don't eat enough, your body will break down muscle for fuel. That's bad.

Target: 0.7–1g of protein per pound of bodyweight per day. If you weigh 170 lbs, aim for 120–170g of protein daily.

High protein foods: chicken breast, ground turkey, eggs, Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, canned tuna, lean beef, protein powder.

Carbs and Fat

After you hit your protein goal and set your calorie target, split the remaining calories between carbs and fat however you prefer. Neither is the enemy.

  • Carbs: Fuel your workouts. Don't cut them too low if you're training hard.
  • Fat: Important for hormones. Don't go below ~0.3g per pound of bodyweight.

A Simple Example

180 lb person cutting at 2000 calories:

  • Protein: 160g = 640 cal
  • Fat: 55g = 495 cal
  • Carbs: 216g = 865 cal

Don't overthink the exact split. Hit your protein, stay in your calories, and you're good.
