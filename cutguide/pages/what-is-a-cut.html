What Is a Cut Phase

The basics — what it is, why it works, and whether you actually need one.

The Short Answer

A cut phase is a period where you eat less than your body burns, forcing it to pull energy from stored fat. That's it. The goal is to lose body fat while keeping as much muscle as possible.

It's not a crash diet. It's not starving yourself. It's a controlled, temporary calorie deficit combined with enough protein and consistent training to protect your muscle.

Key concept: You need to be in a calorie deficit to lose fat. Every effective diet works because of this, regardless of what it's called.

Do You Actually Need to Cut?

If you're a complete beginner, you might be able to lose fat and build muscle at the same time — this is called a body recomposition. A structured cut makes more sense if you already have some muscle and want to lean down, or if you've been eating at maintenance for a while.

If you have a lot of fat to lose and have never trained consistently, just eating at a moderate deficit and lifting will get you solid results without overcomplicating it.

How Long Should It Last?

Most people run cuts for 8–16 weeks. Going longer can hurt your metabolism and make the diet unsustainable mentally. Cut until you hit your goal or until the process stops feeling manageable, then reverse diet out.

Reality check: Sustainable fat loss is about 0.5–1% of bodyweight per week. For most people that's 0.5–1.5 lbs. Faster than that and you're likely losing muscle too.

What You'll Need

  • A daily calorie target
  • A protein goal
  • A consistent training program
  • A food tracking app (MyFitnessPal, Cronometer)
  • Patience — this takes weeks, not days
