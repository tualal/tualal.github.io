Managing Hunger and Cravings

Real strategies for staying on track when you want to eat everything in sight.

Hunger Is Normal — But Manageable

You will feel hungry during a cut. That's part of the deal. The goal isn't to eliminate hunger, it's to make it manageable enough that you can stay consistent without going crazy.

The good news: hunger is largely driven by food volume and habits, not just calories. You can feel more full on the same number of calories by making smarter choices.

High-Volume, Low-Calorie Foods

These foods take up a lot of space in your stomach without costing many calories:

  • Leafy greens (spinach, arugula, romaine) — basically free calories
  • Cucumbers, celery, peppers, zucchini
  • Watermelon and berries
  • Broth-based soups
  • Air-popped popcorn
Strategy: Add a big side salad or a cup of broth to meals. It takes up stomach volume and slows eating pace without adding many calories.

Protein Keeps You Full

Protein is the most satiating macronutrient. If you're constantly hungry, check your protein intake first. Most people who feel miserable on a cut are undereating protein and overeating carbs.

Managing Cravings

Cravings aren't a willpower problem — they're a normal response to restriction. A few things that actually help:

  • Budget calories for one treat per day. A small dessert you plan for is way better than binging after three days of denial.
  • Find lower-calorie swaps for your go-to cravings (diet soda, light ice cream, high-protein versions of snacks).
  • Don't keep problem foods in your house if you can't moderate them.
  • Drink water first — sometimes thirst reads as hunger.

Sleep and Hunger

Sleep deprivation significantly increases hunger hormones (ghrelin). If you're sleeping poorly, you will be hungrier and have less willpower. Fixing your sleep is one of the most impactful things you can do on a cut.
