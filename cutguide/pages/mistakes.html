Common Mistakes

The stuff that trips up beginners on their first cut — and how to avoid it.

Eating Too Little

More aggressive doesn't mean more effective. Cutting calories too hard too fast will cause muscle loss, make you miserable, and usually ends in a binge. A 500 cal deficit is plenty. If you want results faster, stay consistent longer — don't starve yourself.

Not Eating Enough Protein

This is the most common mistake by a wide margin. People focus on eating "clean" and cutting carbs but neglect protein, then wonder why they look flat and weak after losing weight. Hit your protein first, every single day.

Cardio Instead of Lifting

A lot of beginners think they need to do tons of cardio to lose fat. You don't. Diet creates the deficit. Lifting preserves the muscle. Cardio is just a tool to help a little with the deficit — it's not the main event.

Common trap: Doing an hour of cardio every day while skipping the weight room. You'll lose weight but look worse and feel weaker. Lift first.

Inconsistent Tracking

Tracking Monday through Friday and eyeballing the weekends is extremely common and almost always explains a plateau. Weekends are where most people's deficits disappear. You don't have to be obsessive, but you do have to be honest.

Weighing Yourself Wrong

Weighing at random times, after meals, with clothes on, and panicking at daily fluctuations leads to unnecessary stress. Weigh every morning after using the bathroom, no clothes, and track the weekly average. That number is what matters.

Setting Unrealistic Timelines

Expecting to lose 20 lbs in 6 weeks is how people end up doing something extreme, hating the process, and quitting. 1 lb per week is actually solid progress. In 12 weeks that's 12 lbs of mostly fat. That's a noticeable difference.

Quitting After One Bad Day

One bad day doesn't ruin a cut. One week of bad days might. The difference between people who get results and people who don't is usually just showing back up the next day and getting back on track.
