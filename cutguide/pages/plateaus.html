Dealing with Plateaus

What to do when the scale stops moving and you feel stuck.

Why Plateaus Happen

Your body adapts to the deficit over time. As you lose weight, your TDEE drops because you're smaller and your body becomes more metabolically efficient. The deficit that was working in week 2 may no longer be a deficit in week 8.

Also, weight loss is not linear. Day-to-day water fluctuations, hormonal cycles, high-sodium days, and stress can all mask fat loss on the scale for days or even a week at a time. A "plateau" is often just noise.

First check: Are you actually tracking accurately? Most "plateaus" are caused by underestimating intake. Weigh your food for a week and see if anything changes.

How to Break a Real Plateau

If you've been consistent and the scale genuinely hasn't moved in 2–3 weeks, here's what to try:

  • Drop calories by 100–150: Small reduction, not drastic.
  • Add low-intensity cardio: A 30-min daily walk burns 150–200 extra calories and is easy to recover from.
  • Take a diet break: Eat at maintenance for 1–2 weeks. This can reset hunger hormones and often results in more progress after.
  • Check sleep and stress: High cortisol causes water retention and can stall visible progress even when fat is being lost.

What NOT to Do

  • Don't slash calories dramatically. You'll lose muscle and feel terrible.
  • Don't add 2 hours of cardio per day. Not sustainable.
  • Don't switch programs every week. Consistency is the whole game.

The Mindset

Plateaus are temporary and part of the process. Everyone hits them. The people who get results are the ones who adjust and keep going instead of quitting or panicking.
