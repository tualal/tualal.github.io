Building Your Training Schedule

How to structure your workouts during a cut without burning out.

Keep Lifting

The biggest mistake people make during a cut is dropping weight in the gym to "go easier on themselves." That's the wrong move. Resistance training is what tells your body to hold onto muscle. If you stop giving it that signal, it will burn muscle along with fat.

Keep lifting heavy (relative to your level). Don't dramatically change your program just because you're cutting. If anything, cut back on volume slightly if you feel beat up, but keep the intensity.

Goal in the gym during a cut: Maintain strength. If you can keep your lifts around the same weight, you're keeping your muscle.

Cardio — How Much?

Cardio is optional if your diet is dialed in. It helps create a bigger deficit without having to drop food too low, which is especially useful when you're deeper into a cut and already eating pretty lean.

  • Low-intensity (walks, cycling): Easy to recover from. 30–60 min a few times a week is plenty.
  • High-intensity (HIIT, sprints): Burns more calories but takes longer to recover from. 1–2 sessions per week max.

Sample Weekly Structure

MondayLift (upper body or push)

Sleep and Recovery

Sleep is underrated. Poor sleep raises cortisol, which can cause water retention and muscle breakdown. Aim for 7–9 hours. If you're sleeping 5–6 hours consistently, that will slow your progress more than you think.
