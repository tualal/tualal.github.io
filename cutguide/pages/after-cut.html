What to Do After Your Cut

How to reverse diet, keep your results, and figure out what's next.

Don't Just Go Back to Eating Everything

After weeks of eating in a deficit, your metabolism has slowed slightly and your hunger hormones are elevated. If you immediately go back to eating at your old maintenance or above, you'll gain fat fast.

The goal is a reverse diet — slowly adding calories back in over several weeks so your body has time to adjust.

Reverse diet rule: Add 50–100 calories per week until you're back at maintenance. This takes 4–8 weeks but minimizes fat regain.

What to Expect

You'll gain some weight when you increase food. Most of it will be water and glycogen stored in your muscles (carbs pull water into muscle tissue). This is normal and not fat. Your muscles will look fuller and you'll feel stronger in the gym again.

Maintaining Your Results

Maintenance is its own skill. A few things that help:

  • Keep a loose awareness of your intake. You don't need to track forever, but check in when your weight starts creeping.
  • Keep lifting consistently. Your body holds onto muscle when you give it a reason to.
  • Weigh yourself regularly. It's easier to adjust early than after 15 lbs creep back on.

What's Next?

After a cut, most people go into a maintenance or slight surplus phase to rebuild their energy levels and maximize muscle growth. This is sometimes called a "bulk" or just "eating at maintenance."

A typical cycle looks like this: cut for 8–16 weeks, reverse diet, maintenance or bulk for several months, cut again if needed. Over time, each cycle leaves you leaner and more muscular.

You Made It

A successful cut takes discipline, consistency, and patience. If you followed this guide and stuck with it, you earned the results. Take the lessons you learned and carry them into whatever you do next.
