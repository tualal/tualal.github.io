Setting Your Calories

Find your maintenance calories and build a deficit that actually works.

Start with TDEE

TDEE stands for Total Daily Energy Expenditure — basically how many calories your body burns in a day. To lose fat, you need to eat below this number consistently.

There are calculators online that estimate this based on your weight, height, age, and activity level. Use one as a starting point, but know that it's just an estimate. You'll adjust based on real results.

Recommended: Search "TDEE calculator" and use your current weight, height, age, and activity level. Be honest about activity — most people overestimate it.

How Big Should the Deficit Be?

A 300–500 calorie daily deficit is the sweet spot for most people. At 500 calories under maintenance, you're looking at roughly 1 lb of fat loss per week.

Deficit SizeWeekly Loss (approx)Best For
250 cal/day~0.5 lbsLean people, slow cut
500 cal/day~1 lbMost beginners
750+ cal/day1.5+ lbsHigher body fat only

Tracking Your Intake

You don't have to be perfect, but you do have to track. Use a food scale and a logging app. Eyeballing portions is the fastest way to stall progress without knowing why.

Log everything for the first 2–4 weeks until you develop an accurate sense of portion sizes. After that you can ease up a bit if tracking feels obsessive.

Adjusting Over Time

If you're not losing weight after 2 weeks of consistent tracking, drop calories by 100–150. If you're losing too fast (more than 1.5 lbs/week for an extended stretch), add a little back in. Let the results guide you.

Tip: Weigh yourself daily and take a 7-day average. Day-to-day fluctuations from water and food volume are normal and will mess with your head if you take them literally.
