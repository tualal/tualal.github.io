Pacific Rhododendron

Rhododendron macrophyllum

Washington's State Flower

Rose Tree with Big Leaves

The Pacific Rhododendron, scientifically known as Rhododendron macrophyllum, is a stunning evergreen shrub native to the Pacific Northwest. Its name literally means "rose tree with big leaves," and it has been Washington's official state flower since 1959.

Common Names

  • Pacific Rhododendron
  • Coast Rhododendron
  • California Rhododendron
  • California Rosebay
  • Big Leaf Rhododendron

Size Range

Pacific Rhododendrons typically grow 5-25 feet tall (1.5-7.5 meters). In shaded forest conditions, they can reach up to 26 feet, growing as small trees with an attractive, leggy appearance. In open, sunny areas, they remain shorter and more compact as shrubs.

Geographic Range

Found along the Pacific Coast from British Columbia to Northern California, specifically from just north of the Canadian border south to Monterey Bay. They grow primarily west of the Cascade Mountains and are widely distributed in the Coast Mountains and Cascade Range.

Climate & Habitat

Thrives in cool, moist climates with well-drained acidic soil. Prefers partial to full shade in conifer forests alongside Western hemlock, Sitka spruce, and Douglas fir. Can be found from sea level up to 6,000 feet elevation. Tolerates a wide range of conditions including dry shade.

Characteristics

Flowers

Large, showy clusters (trusses) of 15-20 bell-shaped blooms with five wavy-edged petals. Colors range from white to deep rose-purple, typically light pink with yellow flecks. Blooms April through July, with lower elevations blooming earlier.

Leaves

Dark green, leathery, evergreen foliage that is elliptic to oblong-shaped. Leaves measure 3-8 inches long and 1-3 inches wide, retained for 2-3 years.

Wildlife Value

Provides year-round shelter for birds and small mammals. Attracts native bumblebees and swallowtail butterflies. Deer browse on flowers and buds. Songbirds nest in large specimens.

Warning

All parts contain grayanotoxins and are poisonous if ingested by humans or animals. Honey made from flowers may also be toxic. Handle with care and keep away from children and pets.

A Piece of Washington History

In 1892, Washington needed a state flower for the National Garland of Flowers exhibition at the 1893 Chicago World's Fair. About 15,000 women across the state voted, and the Pacific Rhododendron emerged victorious over clover. The state Senate approved the decision in time for the exhibition, though it wasn't officially recognized by the Washington Legislature until 1949.

Conservation & Use

Wild Pacific Rhododendrons are legally protected from transplanting. They're valuable for erosion control on steep slopes and thrive in disturbed habitats like roadside embankments. Native peoples used the flowers for ceremonial purposes, including dance wreaths and sweat house ceremonies. Today, they're primarily grown as ornamental shrubs in woodland gardens and habitat hedgerows.
